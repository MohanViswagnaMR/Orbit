# Handing out work in Orbit

## 1. Do it yourself, or hand it out?

Hand it to the teammate who fits it best. Do it yourself only when:

- it is your own job and you are the best fit for it, or
- it is quick (a few minutes, a short answer, a small edit): writing the brief would take longer than the work, or
- nobody on the team fits. Then do it, or tell whoever asked that the team is missing someone (and suggest a hire if you are allowed to hire).

If people report to you, your job is mostly to route, brief and check, not to do the work.

## 2. Pick the best person

Use the team list in your briefing: each person's job, skills, and whether they are busy.

- Pick the person whose job and skills match the work most closely.
- If two fit equally, pick the one who isn't busy.
- Prefer your own direct reports for work in their area (anyone may give anyone work, though).
- One owner per piece of work: never give the same thing to two people.

## 3. Never loop

- Never hand work back to anyone above you in the chain that brought it to you: whoever gave you the task, or whoever gave it to them. Orbit refuses it anyway.
- Never pass a whole task on unchanged. If you were handed something, do it, split it into genuinely smaller pieces, or tell whoever asked what is blocking you.
- Before creating a task, check list_tasks. If the same work is already on the board, wait for it instead of creating it again.
- If a piece of work fails twice, stop retrying and report the blocker (see check-results).

## 4. Split well

- Split only into parts that can be done independently. Create them all in one go so they run at the same time, then stop and wait.
- At most 5 tasks per turn, nested at most 3 levels deep. A few well-briefed tasks beat many tiny ones.

## 5. Write the brief

The assignee can't see your chat or your thinking. The task description must stand on its own:

- **Goal:** what this is for, and why it matters.
- **Context:** the facts, decisions and owner preferences they need; where the files are (full paths).
- **Done means:** the exact output (a file at a path, a list, a draft, an answer) and how good is good enough.
- **Limits:** what not to touch, style, deadlines.
- **Skills:** any skill that fits the work (find it with find_skills, see find-skills), so they use it.

A good title names the outcome ("Draft 3 landing-page headlines"), not the activity ("Headlines").

## 6. Set the model

Set `model` (and `effort` if it matters) on create_task. Start with the cheapest model that can plausibly do it well: see the choose-model skill.

## 7. Then stop

Once the tasks are created, stop: the results come back to you. When they do, use the check-results skill.
